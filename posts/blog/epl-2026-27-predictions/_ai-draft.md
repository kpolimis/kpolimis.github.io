## The Title Race: Arsenal Lead the Way

After ending their 22-year title drought last season, Arsenal enter 2026/27 as the clear favorites at +120. Manchester City sit second at +350, with Liverpool (+550), Chelsea (+800), and Manchester United (+900) rounding out the top five contenders.

But raw odds can be misleading. To really understand what the market thinks, we need to convert those American odds into implied probabilities. Here's the key insight: the probabilities don't sum to 100%. They sum to about 114%. That extra 14.3% is the bookmaker's margin, often called the "vig" or "overround." Once we normalize it away, we get a cleaner picture of the true probabilities:

| Club | Odds | Fair Probability |
|------|------|------------------|
| Arsenal | +120 | 39.8% |
| Manchester City | +350 | 19.4% |
| Liverpool | +550 | 13.5% |
| Chelsea | +800 | 9.7% |
| Manchester United | +900 | 8.8% |
| Tottenham | +2200 | 3.8% |
| Aston Villa | +5000 | 1.7% |

The takeaway? Arsenal are favorites, but a 40% chance is far from a sure thing. The market is essentially saying there's a 60% chance someone else lifts the trophy.

## How Accurate Are Preseason Favorites, Really?

This is where it gets interesting. I pulled the last ten seasons of data to see how often the preseason favorite actually won the title. The results might surprise you.

- The preseason favorite won the title **6 out of the last 10 seasons**.
- In the 4 seasons they didn't win, the favorite still finished in the top 3 every single time — except once.
- That exception was last season, when defending champions Liverpool were installed as favorites and finished a shocking 5th, while Arsenal broke through.

So the lesson is clear: preseason favorites are usually right, or at least close. Betting against them is betting against history. But football, as we all know, is a game of glorious unpredictability. Just ask Leicester City, whose 5000-1 title in 2015/16 remains the greatest upset in sports history.
